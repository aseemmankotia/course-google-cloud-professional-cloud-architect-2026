## Chapter 12: Full Exam Simulation & 120-Minute Time-Management Strategy — Practice Questions

**Q1.** During the exam you encounter a case study you haven't seen before with 6 questions attached. You've used 40 minutes on the first 20 standalone questions. What is the BEST approach for the case-study block?

A) Skip all 6 questions and mark them for review at the very end
B) Read each question first, then search the case study for just the relevant paragraph before answering each one
C) Skim the 6 question stems first to know what to look for, then read the case study once fully, holding the context while answering all 6 before moving on
D) Answer based on general Google Cloud best practice without reading the case study, since case studies rarely add new constraints

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

One focused read of the case study, primed by knowing what the questions ask, lets you hold constraints in working memory and answer all linked questions efficiently without re-reading.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Skipping entirely risks running out of time later and losing the context you'd have built early
- Re-searching the document per question multiplies total reading time far beyond one focused pass
- Case studies always encode specific constraints (budget, compliance, SLA) that override generic best practice and must be read

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q2.** Which elimination technique is MOST effective for a single-answer scenario question where two options both technically satisfy the stated requirement?

A) Choose the option listed first, since exam option order is effectively random
B) Re-read the question stem for the specific optimization keyword (cost, latency, compliance, managed overhead) and eliminate the option that doesn't align with it
C) Always choose the option using fewer managed services to minimize operational overhead
D) Choose the more expensive, enterprise-grade option, since Google Cloud exams favor larger solutions

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Exam stems always embed a decision keyword tied to what 'best' means in that scenario; re-scanning for it breaks ties between two otherwise valid options.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Option order carries no signal about correctness
- Minimizing ops overhead is a common bias but not universal — some questions explicitly ask for lowest cost even at the expense of more services
- There is no bias toward more expensive or 'bigger' solutions on this exam

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q3.** You are 90 minutes into a 120-minute exam with 15 questions remaining, including 3 flagged multiple-select questions, and only about 20 minutes left. What is the BEST time allocation?

A) Spend all 20 remaining minutes only on the 3 flagged multi-select questions since they are worth more points
B) Divide remaining time so every unflagged question gets a quick answer first (~1.3 min each), then apply leftover time to the flagged questions
C) Submit the exam immediately to avoid running out of time
D) Spend 15 minutes on the flagged questions and skip the remaining 12 unflagged questions entirely

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

An unanswered question is a guaranteed zero, while even a rushed answer has nonzero expected value; guarantee coverage of all remaining questions first, then spend leftover time on harder flagged items.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Multi-select questions are not weighted higher than single-answer questions, so this guarantees zero on 12 questions
- Submitting early forfeits guessing value on unanswered questions with no benefit
- This leaves 12 guaranteed zeros for no proven point advantage

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q4.** On the Professional Cloud Architect exam, how are multiple-select questions scored relative to single-answer questions?

A) All correct options must be selected with none incorrect to receive credit, and they carry the same weight as single-answer questions
B) They award partial credit for each correct option selected
C) They are weighted at double the point value of single-answer questions
D) They are optional and can be skipped without any scoring penalty

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Multiple-select questions use all-or-nothing scoring and count the same as any other question — there is no partial credit and no extra weighting.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- No partial credit is given for partially correct selections
- There is no official point multiplier for multi-select items
- Skipping any question, multi-select or not, counts as an automatic miss

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q5.** You've flagged 8 questions using the exam interface's flag feature, but with 10 minutes left you can only revisit 4. Which criterion should determine review priority?

A) Revisit questions in the order they were originally flagged
B) Revisit the standalone (non-case-study-linked) questions you're least confident about first, since case-study questions require re-reading the full scenario again
C) Revisit only the multiple-select questions since they are lower point value otherwise
D) Revisit the questions that originally took the longest to answer

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Standalone flagged items can be resolved quickly from recall alone, giving the best expected value per minute when time is scarce; case-study items cost extra time to re-contextualize.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Chronological flag order has no relationship to difficulty or expected payoff
- Multiple-select questions are not lower-value — this premise is false
- Original time spent doesn't predict whether revisiting will change the answer

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q6.** What is the recommended per-question time budget baseline for a 120-minute exam with an expected 50-60 questions, before adjusting for case-study blocks?

A) Exactly 1 minute per question with no exceptions
B) About 5 minutes per question given the depth of technical analysis required
C) Approximately 2 to 2.4 minutes per question on average, with buffer minutes reserved for case-study sections
D) No budgeting is needed since the platform only auto-submits at the hard stop

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

120÷50 = 2.4 min and 120÷60 = 2.0 min, so the baseline pace is roughly 2-2.4 minutes per question, with extra buffer set aside for the slower case-study blocks.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- 1 minute per question ignores the extra reading time case studies require and is unsustainably tight
- 5 minutes per question would only allow 24 questions in 120 minutes, well under the expected count
- Ignoring pacing risks leaving questions unanswered, which count as automatic misses

</details>

**Q7.** During a full practice exam review you find you missed 6 of 8 questions tagged to 'Designing for security and compliance' but only 2 of 10 in 'Designing for reliability.' What should be your NEXT study action?

A) Retake the exact same full practice exam immediately to reinforce all domains equally
B) Focus targeted restudy on the security and compliance domain first, since it shows the largest gap, then re-test with domain-specific questions before another full simulation
C) Skip the security domain since it represents only a small fraction of the exam
D) Move directly to case-study review only, since domain-level accuracy isn't diagnostic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Post-exam review should map misses back to domains and prioritize restudy on the domain with the largest error rate before another full-length simulation.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Retaking the identical exam doesn't target the specific weak domain and risks memorizing answers rather than concepts
- Security and compliance is one of the officially weighted domains, not a negligible slice
- Domain-level miss rates are precisely the signal needed to direct restudy efficiently

</details>

**Q8.** Given that the exam draws from four published case studies, which of the following is the BEST use of final review time before exam day?

A) Memorize the entire text of all four case studies verbatim
B) Re-read each case study once and create a one-page summary covering company background, business requirements, technical requirements, and existing environment constraints for each
C) Skip case study review entirely since the full case study text is provided during the exam
D) Only review the case study that appeared most often in past practice exams and ignore the other three

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

A one-page constraint summary per case study saves significant reading/parsing time on exam day and reduces the risk of missing a stated constraint under time pressure, even though full text is provided during the test.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Verbatim memorization is unnecessary and inefficient since the text is provided during the exam
- While the text is provided, being unfamiliar with it costs valuable minutes reading cold under time pressure
- All four case studies remain in current rotation and any of them could appear

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>
